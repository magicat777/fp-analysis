---
name: Repair cost = % of equipment value
description: FP repair costs scale as a percentage of the equipment's purchase value, so expensive saltwater/E-rod gear is brutally costly to repair compared to budget gear
type: feedback
---

**Rule:** Repair costs in Fishing Planet are a percentage of the equipment's total purchase value. Expensive gear = expensive repairs. Same overcrank that costs $5k on a freshwater reel can cost $50-100k on a saltwater E-reel.

**Why:** Validated 2026-04-29 at Skarland Fjord first trip. User wore down 2 of 3 E-reels (Glacion 21000 SE, Arcspark 2W 22000 SE, AmperDrive X 24000 SE) by overcranking on the opening hour of fights. On a $540k haul (24 fish), net credit gain was only +$86k - approximately $390k vanished into repair costs alone.

**Scope:** Applies broadly to all gear categories, not just rods/reels:
- Tackle boxes
- Vests
- Stringers / keepnets
- Boats (especially Valkyrie, Chamaeleon Cruiser tier)
- Rods, reels, lines (obvious)

**How to apply when coaching:**

1. **Saltwater/endgame trips need finesse from minute one.** Don't wait for a near-broken reel before throttling back - by then the repair bill is already crushing. Open with reel speed 1-2 + tension just under the fish's pull strength on every fight, regardless of fish size.

2. **Watch reel durability mid-trip, not just at end.** Pull a rod the moment a reel hits 70-80% durability. The relationship between durability and repair cost is non-linear at the high end - going from 95% → 70% may cost less than 70% → 50%.

3. **Don't recommend buying premium gear just because the user *can*.** Premium gear pays back via productivity, but only if repair burn is controlled. A worn-down AmperDrive X 24000 SE may cost more to repair than a fresh GiantSpin 9000 SE costs to buy.

4. **Multi-day trips amortize repairs better than single-day.** One repair cycle covers multiple sessions. Single-day saltwater trips often net negative once repairs hit.

5. **Calculate true profit, not gross fish sale.** The user reads "took home $500k" as gross; the actual credit delta after license + travel + repairs is the real number. Always lead with net for trip post-mortems.

**Skarland-specific rule of thumb:** Plan for ~50-70% of gross fish sales to be eaten by repairs on the first 1-2 trips while learning the location. Drops to ~20-30% once finesse retrieve becomes muscle memory.
