**UPDATED 2026-05-02 — Bass Challenge contest validation:**

The in-game **XP rating band is the diagnostic readout** for whether your gear is balanced. Validated at Everglades Bass Challenge (31 catches / 30 min, 130 lb, ranked 31/221 = top 14%):

- **+3 to -1 XP rating = ideal match** — gear matches fish class, sustainable contest performance
- **-3 XP rating = overpowered** — gear catches fish but penalized; WBT casting/spinning templates and other casting setups consistently showed -3 even when hooking up at decent rates
- **Hit rate alone does NOT win contests** — overpowered gear can have a slightly better hook rate but the XP penalty kills your overall standing

**Counterintuitive call-out: World Bass Tour (WBT) branded rods are NOT automatically optimal for bass contests.** They were too short on distance AND had wrong recovery speed (too fast OR too slow) AND showed -3 XP even when matched with appropriate reels. WBT being branded "bass" doesn't mean it's tuned for finesse line weights. AuroraSport + Sport spinning reel was also too short on casting distance.

**Contest-validated MVP for 5-10 lb bass (Everglades):**
- JigWinner 8'10" SE (Light, 1/6-5/8 Oz, 3-13 Lb, Fast)
- Prima 3500 SE (31.5"/turn — medium-class recovery)
- Braid .008" (10 Lb) + Fluorocarbon Leader .011" (9.9 Lb)
- Bass Jig 1/2 Oz #3/0 + Shad 3" trailer
- Retrieve: speed 3 (med-fast) initial cast → speed 2 (medium) maintenance, trigger pulses to bounce off bottom
- Result: ~95% of casts produced a hit, +3 to -1 XP throughout, handles Unique-class fights with finesse

**UPDATED 2026-04-27:** What started as a "triangle" is actually a **quad** — reel recovery rate is the fourth variable, validated through Everglades Largemouth Bass testing. Recovery (inches/turn) × in-game speed setting = effective lure swim velocity. If recovery is too low, you can't reach productive speed even at max setting; if too high, you blow through the strike zone at low settings.

**Validated recovery threshold for Largemouth Bass at Everglades (1/2 Oz jig + lily pad cover):**
- Helios 2000S (21.5"/turn): TOO SLOW — required cranking reel speed to max, killed jig action
- Basilisk 3500P / FatBoySpin 6000 SE (29.5"/turn): workable but middling
- Prima 3500 SE (31.5"/turn): sweet spot at speed 2-3 — CONTEST-VALIDATED 2026-05-02
- Big River 6000 SE (47.2"/turn): expected to be optimal — more casts/min

**Rule of thumb:** for jig/finesse retrieves, target reel recovery 30-45"/turn. For burn lures (buzzbait, spinnerbait at speed), 35-50"/turn. Always pair recovery with reel-speed setting that puts the LURE in the right water column — not the cosmetic gear ratio.



User's tactical insight (2026-04-26): **Three variables interact to determine hit-to-cast rate at any given location. They are interdependent  -  getting two right and one wrong tanks the whole rig from ~50% to 0%.**

The triangle:

1. **Lure weight**  -  must keep the lure off the seabed (too heavy = sinks, snags) but reach proper depth (too light = drifts, no presentation)
2. **Retrieve speed**  -  must keep the lure off the seabed at chosen depth (too slow = sinks; too fast = wrong action / fish can't commit)
3. **Line strength/type**  -  must handle fish class without breaking AND not affect lure action (too heavy/stiff = lure flexes wrong; too light = breaks)

**Why this matters:**
- Most FP advice focuses on ONE variable at a time
- Real productivity comes from BALANCING all three for the location's depth + fish class + lure type
- A "perfect" rod + reel + hook can still produce 0 fish if line is wrong for the conditions
- This explains why two players with seemingly identical setups can have very different results

**How to apply:**

When recommending a setup, ALWAYS explicitly address all three:
- **Lure weight**  -  match to depth target and fish class (1/5 Oz shallow, 1 Oz medium, 2+ Oz deep)
- **Retrieve speed**  -  match to lure type and water column position (slow for soft plastics, medium for spinners, fast for buzzbaits)
- **Line type + weight**  -  match to fish class minimum AND to lure-type behavior (avoid fluoro on active retrieves where flex matters)

Don't say just "use 1 Oz spoon on SharpCaster." Say "1 Oz spoon + 25-40 Lb braid + medium-paced stop-and-go retrieve at 2/3 depth on SharpCaster."

**The frustration factor:**
User describes this as "the most frustrating thing in the game"  -  getting MOST right but one wrong component tanks the rate. This frustration is informative: it means our recommendations need to be COMPLETE (rod + reel + line + lure + hook + retrieve technique), not partial. Missing one variable in the recommendation reproduces the user's frustration.
