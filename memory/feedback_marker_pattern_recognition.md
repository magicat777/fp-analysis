User's tactical insight (2026-04-27 at Everglades): **Boat markers in FP are placed where fish were caught. The PATTERN of markers across an area reveals fish behavior.** Trophy fish often hold on the EDGES of structure features rather than the center.

**The "live on the edge" pattern:**
- Markers form a circle/ring around a deep zone
- Center of the ring is empty (deep water with no markers)
- Trophy fish prefer the perimeter where deep meets shallow (edge transition)
- Casting INTO the empty center = no bites
- Casting along the marker ring perimeter = trophy strikes

**Why this works:**
- Edges are ambush points  -  fish drop into deep water for safety, rise to shallows to feed
- Predators (Trophy Largemouth Bass, etc.) patrol the transition zone
- Prey species concentrate on shelves and drop-offs near the deep water for cover access

**How to apply when coaching:**

1. **Read the marker pattern** before casting blindly:
   - Solid cluster of markers = active feeding zone (cast directly)
   - Ring around empty center = edge-fishing pattern (cast on perimeter)
   - Scattered markers = no consistent pattern (try multiple spots)
   - Linear chain = weed line or channel edge (work parallel to the line)

2. **For trophy hunting at known-good locations:**
   - Look for marker patterns that imply structure (depth changes)
   - Cast ON or JUST INSIDE the marker ring
   - Don't waste casts in the empty center

3. **Use the boat fish-finder to confirm:**
   - Check depth at center of marker ring (usually deepest point)
   - Check depth at perimeter (transition slope)
   - Trophy fish hold at the slope, not the bottom

4. **Combine with bait/lure choice:**
   - Slow-fall presentations (1/2 Oz jig at speed 2-3) for edge-feeding
   - Pause longer at the deep edge
   - Hop the lure UP toward shallows to trigger ambush strikes
