On 2026-04-26 user tested line strength range from 23 Lb to 40 Lb at Blue Crab Island across three line types (mono, braid, fluorocarbon).

**Findings:**
- **Hit rate did NOT differ much** across the 23-40 Lb range  -  line strength didn't change bite frequency at BCI's fish class
- **Line type matters more than weight:**
  - **Mono**  -  neutral, casts well, stretchy
  - **Braid**  -  neutral, low stretch, good for retrieves
  - **Fluorocarbon**  -  **flexed significantly more, made retrieves much more difficult**

**Why:** In deeper water with current/structure (BCI southern inlet, Mokonzi feeder spots), fluorocarbon's stiffness varies with depth/temperature. Combined with active-retrieve rods (SharpCaster spoons, DragonCast jigs), the flex creates inconsistent lure action and harder hooksets.

**How to apply:**
- **At BCI:** recommend braid or mono in 25-40 Lb range for active rods (Slot 2/3/4)
- **Avoid fluorocarbon on retrieve-heavy active rods at BCI**  -  fine for bottom rigs and leader sections, but not main line
- **Don't oversell line-weight upgrades**  -  bumping from 25 Lb to 40 Lb won't increase hit rate at BCI; pick what suits the technique
- **For other locations** (Congo, Saint-Croix, Dnipro), the same pattern likely applies but needs separate validation
- **Fluoro still wins** for clear-water leader sections (its invisibility overrides flex concerns at the last 1-2 feet)

**What NOT to recommend:**
- "Use 40 Lb fluoro main line for more hits"  -  wrong, hit rate same as 25 Lb
- "Switch to fluoro on the SharpCaster active rig"  -  flex hurts more than invisibility helps
