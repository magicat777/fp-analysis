# 2026-04-23 AM  -  Congo River  -  Finesse Retrieve Discovery

## Session Summary

- **Level:** 52
- **Location:** Congo River
- **Setup:** NileChasseur 6'3" SE + Three-way rig + 1 Oz Drop Sinker + Offset Hook #6 + Ribbed Shad 6"
- **Key finding:** **Finesse retrieve required for 100+ lb Nile Perch  -  brute force destroys reels**

## The Problem

Hitting Perch on **every cast** with the three-way rig + Ribbed Shad  -  excellent bite rate. But trying to land the bigger Nile Perch (100+ lb) with maximum reel speed and tension was catastrophic:

- **First 2 catches:** Broke reel trying to brute-force the fight
- **Wear rate:** ~1% per second at maximum reel/tension
- **Total reel life at max:** ~100 seconds before destruction
- **Cost:** Broken reels require baitcoins (expensive) or large cash repairs AFTER leaving the area

## The Finesse Technique (4th catch)

On the 4th large fish, switched approach. This is now the documented method for Congo 100+ lb fish:

### Line Tension
- **Keep tension JUST UNDER the fish's pull strength**
- Allow some slack on the line so the fish can run if it chooses
- Avoid maximum drag / locked tension  -  this triggers maximum fish aggression

### Reel Speed
- **Low to Low-Medium only** (speed 1 to 2 on the scale)
- Do NOT reel at maximum speed  -  this also aggros the fish
- Slow, steady, patient retrieval

### Goal
**Do not push the fish into maximum aggression.** When a big Nile Perch goes aggro:
- It pulls to maximum force
- It runs hundreds of yards of line
- It wears down the reel rapidly
- It breaks tackle

### Outcome Priority
- **Preferred failure:** Broken tackle (hook, line, leader)  -  cheap replacement
- **Avoid failure:** Broken reel  -  expensive (baitcoins) or requires end-of-trip cash repair at a distant home base

## Tackle Weight Doesn't Save You

Tested lesser tackle on a big fish  -  it still caused reel wear AND broke the line. This confirms:
- **Brute force fails at any tackle weight** when the fish goes aggro
- **Finesse is universal**  -  technique matters more than gear for 100+ lb Congo fish
- The tackle ceiling hypothesis was wrong (documented 4/21), but even with the right rig, **how you fight the fish** is the final variable

## Retrieve Protocol (Summary)

1. Cast three-way rig + Ribbed Shad to target zone
2. Wait for strike (perch + larger fish all bite this)
3. On hookup, assess fish size by initial pull
4. **If small/medium:** standard retrieve
5. **If 100+ lb feel (strong sustained pull):**
   - Drop drag/tension to ~80-90% of fish's pull
   - Reel speed 1-2 only
   - Let fish run when it wants
   - Regain line only when fish pauses
   - Accept that the fight will take 3-5× longer than brute-force attempt
   - Accept potential tackle loss as cheaper than reel loss

## Key Takeaways

1. **Finesse retrieve is mandatory for Congo 100+ lb fish**  -  brute force destroys reels
2. **Reel wear rate at max:** 1%/second  -  about 100 seconds until destruction
3. **Repair economy:** Broken tackle = cheap, broken reel = expensive (baitcoins or cash repair)
4. **Rig + technique > tackle weight**  -  the 4/21 Unique Kamba 272 lb and the previous Nile Perch 257 lb were both landed using the finesse approach (even if not formally documented then)
5. **Three-way rig + Ribbed Shad 6" = universal Congo producer**  -  confirmed on Perch this session too (every cast)
