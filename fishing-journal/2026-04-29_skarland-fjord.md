## What Worked

**Giant Tiger Prawn = the universal Skarland producer.** It hooked the 636 lb Greenland Shark, the 409 lb Unique Halibut, both Porbeagles, both Young Greenlands, the Unique Common Ling, and most of the cod/ling filler. By weight, Giant Tiger Prawn caught more than every other bait combined.

**E-rod bottom fleet (Slots 5-7) is the right call.** All trophy/unique-class fish came on the three saltwater bottom rigs. Glacion (172 Lb), Arcspark 2W (243 Lb), AmperDrive X (315 Lb) drag classes covered the full ocean fish weight range from cod up through 600+ lb shark.

**Hook size + bait depth correlate with fish size.** Bigger Tungsten Golden #12/0 + deeper drop = bigger fish. Smaller hooks at shallower depths produced 20-30 lb cod/ling filler.

**Stationary bottom rods + 1 active casting rod is the right pace.** ~2-4 minutes between bites, ~5-10 minute reel time on big fish - 4-8 catches/hour with three E-rods deployed and one active rod for cover work.

**Saltwater is XP-dense.** ~2x Congo's XP rate. Cleared first Grandpa's Catch mission (initial intro chain).

## What Didn't Work

**Overcranking the reels in the first hour cost a fortune.** Burned 2 of 3 E-reels down significantly before getting the deep-fight rhythm. **Repair costs are a percentage of equipment value** - the more expensive the gear, the more it costs to fix. Same applies to tackle boxes, vests, stringers/keepnets, and boats. Net credit gain only +$86k on a $540k haul because of repair burn.

**Chamaeleon Cruiser is fun but wrong boat for Skarland.** Doesn't handle the rough water. 2 rod holders per player vs Valkyrie's 3 = 33% less productive deployment. Use it on calmer locations.

**Letting big fish off when overmatched is the right call.** When you hook one too big for your setup, no technique saves you - loosen slack on the line and set the rod in the holder until it tears off. Trying to fight a 600+ lb shark on the wrong reel = guaranteed reel destruction at game-equipment-value % repair cost.

## Tactical Insights for Skarland

1. **Time per 100 ft = ~1 minute reel.** Big fish hit at 500-900 ft depth. A 600+ lb fish at 800 ft = ~8 minute fight just to get it boatside.

2. **Big fish are unstoppable without matched gear.** No combination of finesse, drag tweaks, or line management compensates for a rod/reel that's underweight for the fish class. Match-or-release.

3. **Repair-cost-as-% mechanic:** Cheap freshwater gear can be brute-forced; saltwater E-rod gear cannot. The same overcrank that costs $5k on a freshwater reel costs $50-100k on an E-reel. Finesse retrieve from the start, not after a breakage.

4. **Travel cost recovery requires multi-day stay.** $60k travel + $1,900 license = $61,900 baseline. One ~$540k storage fills makes the trip economic. Multi-day extension multiplies value.

5. **Drop mechanic worked as predicted:** cast → release spool → tungsten sinker freefall → "thunk" → reel up to target depth. Used this on PulseWave + Chillarc + Sigma F effectively.
