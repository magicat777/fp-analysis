### Line Weight Experiment
Tested line strength range from **23 Lb to 40 Lb** across multiple types:
- **Mono**  -  neutral
- **Braid**  -  neutral
- **Fluorocarbon**  -  **flexed significantly more, made retrieves much more difficult**

**Findings:**

## Buzzbait Technique Discovery (CORRECTED)

**Buzzbait 1 Oz #5/0 + Larva 4" / Worm 5" on SharpCaster + VorteX 7000 SE = MVP combo.**

Important correction: FP buzzbaits are **NOT topwater lures** as in real-world fishing. They are **spinner-type lures with a clacker and soft bait attachment**, fished mid-water through bottom.

**Technique at BCI:**
1. Cast and let lure sink toward bottom
2. Bounce off bottom with small lift
3. Raise for a few seconds (mid-water hold)
4. Let it drop back down
5. Bounce a bit off bottom
6. Repeat

**Hookset rule:** Pause LONGER than usual before setting  -  fast hookset snaps the lure out of the fish's mouth. Wait for clear weight commitment.

This is the same drop-and-pause logic that works for Congo three-way Ribbed Shad and Kaniq spoon-salmon retrieves. **Bottom-feeders strike on the drop, not the rise.**

## The Lure Balance Triangle (insight)

User's tactical insight from this trip: three variables must all be balanced for productive fishing  -  getting two right and one wrong tanks hit rate from ~50% to 0%.

1. **Lure weight**  -  keep off seabed but reach proper depth
2. **Retrieve speed**  -  too slow = sinks, too fast = wrong action
3. **Line type + weight**  -  handle fish class without breaking AND not affect lure action

This is now permanent guidance: any rig recommendation must address all three components, not just one or two. The frustration of "everything looked right but nothing worked" is usually one of these three out of balance.
